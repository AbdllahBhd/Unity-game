## 2. The frame: the hearth

Every session opens on the hearth (*lkanun*) in the big room of the old house in Ath
Abbas on Yennayer night. The lamp is lit. Jida, the grandmother, sits with the children.

- **Opening (every night):**
  - Jida: *Amacahu!*
  - Children: *Macahu!*
  - Jida: *Rebbi ad tt-yesselhu, ad teɣzif am usaru.* ("May God make it fine, and long as a thread.")
- **Closing (every tale):** *Tamacahut-iw tedda s iɣzer, nekk ddiɣ s ubrid.* ("My tale went down the ravine, and I went down the road.") Then, pointing at the door: "And that is why we keep the lamp lit."
- Between stages, one short line from Jida or a child's question replaces the Owl's
  remarks. The children are the player's voice: "Jida, did he get out?" "Wait."
- On the first night only, Jida names the hero: "He was no bigger than your fist, and
  cleverer than all of us. They called him Mqidec."

Jida has three moods on screen, like the Owl had three eye states: **telling** (calm),
**teasing** (a smile; when Teryel is near), **hushed** (leaning in; the dangerous part).
A child's silhouette by the fire reacts: jumps at the hushed parts, laughs at the teasing.

## 3. The hero and the ogress

**Mqidec** is the rabbit of the house. He cannot speak; Jida speaks for him. He is small,
quick and clever: he never beats Teryel by force, he escapes her by wit and by using what
is in the room. In the game that is the puzzle objects.

**Teryel** is the ogress of the tales: huge, hungry, slow to understand and quick to
rage. She lives above the village in a house of her own, with her loom and her kitchen,
and she wants to eat him. She never dies. At the end of each tale she is tricked,
trapped or asleep, and Mqidec is home before the lamp goes out. Her spider form from the
current art stays as her shadow in play; her true shape appears in the hearth scenes as a
tall silhouette with long hair and glowing eyes.

Rule of the tales: **every escape uses a trick, never a fight.** The boss stage of a
tale is a puzzle that traps her (a pot over her head, her own thread round her feet, the
gate dropped behind her), not three hits.

## 4. The tales

Each tale: 4 stages. Stage 1 introduces the place and the new hunters, stage 2 the new
mechanic alone, stage 3 the mechanic combined, stage 4 the trap that beats Teryel.
Three stars per stage stay: each hunter broken is a star; the door always ends the stage.

### Tale 1 — Taxxamt n Teryel (Teryel's kitchen)

*Why he is there:* On Yennayer eve Teryel stole the house's jar of lamp oil, so no lamp
can be lit at midnight. Mqidec slips into her kitchen while she sleeps by the fire to take
it back. **Goal object: the oil jar.**

- **Place:** the kitchen of the ogress's house: earth floor, the hearth, the big grain
  jars (*ikufan*) along the wall, shelves of pots, the loft above.
- **Hunters: tisilin**, her living pots and jars, painted with the pottery signs, eyes
  glowing in their mouths. They hop and roll after him and shatter into shards.
- **Mechanic:** rolling things. Pots roll off shelves, a jar bowls over others, the
  hearth pots (cannons) throw him up to the loft.
- **Stages:** 1 *Ikufan* (the jar wall) · 2 *Tasilt* (the rolling pot) · 3 *Lkanun* (the
  hearth throws him) · 4 *Teryel teṭṭes* (Teryel asleep): roll the great jar down onto her
  and slip out under the door.
- **Jida's lines (drafts):** "She snored like the wind in the gorge." · "A clever one
  does not run. He looks at the room." · "He rolled the jar. Remember the jar."

### Tale 2 — Taxxamt n uzeṭṭa (the loom room)

*Why he is there:* The lamp itself is gone: Teryel has woven it into her loom to keep its
light for herself. Mqidec goes back for it. The threads run from wall to wall, and only the
loom room leads to the roof. **Goal object: the lamp.**

- **Place:** the room of the vertical loom, threads in every direction, the weaving
  stones hanging, the comb and the shuttle.
- **Hunters:** her **spiders** on the threads, and the threads themselves: sticky ones
  catch, tight ones throw.
- **Mechanic:** silk. Sticky web (hold once), tight web (throw), zip thread (slide),
  stones swinging on threads.
- **Stages:** 1 *Lḥarir* (sticky) · 2 *Tasfift* (tight) · 3 *Asaru aɣezfan* (the long
  thread) · 4 *Teryel deg uzeṭṭa* (Teryel in the loom): lead her into her own threads
  until she is wound up like a shuttle, and go out through the roof tiles.
- **Jida's lines:** "A loom has a soul, they say. Hers had a temper." · "The thread
  holds once. Once." · "She chased him into her own weaving. Imagine it."

### Tale 3 — Tiwura n wuzzal (the Iron Gates)

*Why he is there:* With the oil and the lamp, the way home runs through the Bibans pass
at night. Teryel follows him down into the gorge, and the gorge is alive. **Goal: carry the
lit lamp home without it going out** (a death puts it out; the stage restarts).

- **Place:** the cliffs and the river of the Iron Gates below Ath Abbas, moonlight,
  wind in the narrows, loose rocks, the old road.
- **Hunters: ijenniwen**, the night spirits of the springs and the old trees, wisps of
  smoke with eyes that drift along the river and flare when they smell him.
- **Mechanic:** stone and wind. Updrafts in the narrows (engine `wind`), the balanced
  stone (seesaw), the frayed stones that fall, stones that crumble (fade).
- **Stages:** 1 *Aḍu* (the wind) · 2 *Aẓru yettwaktalen* (the balanced stone) · 3 *Iɣzer*
  (the ravine) · 4 *Tawwurt* (the gate): drop the old gate stone behind him with the
  seesaw and leave Teryel on the far side, shouting.
- **Jida's lines:** "The gorge has its own people. Do not whistle there at night." ·
  "He was lighter than the wind, and the wind knew it." · "She is still shouting, they
  say, when the wind comes from the south."

### Tale 4 — Ssqef n Lqelɛa (the rooftops of Lqelɛa)

*Why he is there:* Teryel stole the Yennayer bread from the village oven and hid it on
the highest roof of the citadel. **Goal: the bread (aɣrum).**

- **Hunters:** her ravens (ijeɣdan).
- **Mechanic:** sloped roofs of loose tiles that Mqidec slides down (tap to hop off), and
  stacks of tiles knocked over the edge by a side hit or a dash.
- **Stages:** 13 *Iqermuden* · 14 *Ijeɣdan* · 15 *Ssqef* · 16 *Teryel ɣef ssqef*: slide
  into the last stack so it sends her down the chimney.

### Tale 5 — Tamɛeṣert (the olive press)

*Why he is there:* Jida's own silver bridal brooch, a round **tabzimt** with red coral
and blue enamel, was taken by Teryel, who loves what shines, and hidden in the old olive
press. **Goal: the tabzimt.** The tale opens with Jida remembering her wedding day.

- **Place:** the village press in winter, working day and night: dry-stone walls, the
  screw press and its stacked mats, oil jars, a hanging lamp.
- **Hunters:** her rats (iɣerdayen). They run along their own beam only, or sleep curled up.
- **Mechanic:** spilt oil (a fast landing glides on to the end of the beam, into a rat or
  off the edge; a gentle one stops) and the press wheel, whose level planks carry Mqidec
  round; he launches from the top.
- **Stages:** 17 *Zzit* (the oil) · 18 *Tasirt* (the wheel) · 19 *Azemmur* (wheel and oil
  together) · 20 *Teryel deg tmɛeṣert*: glide along the oily beam into the great press
  beam; it rolls off and pins her.
- **Ending:** Mqidec carries the tabzimt home in his mouth like a cat with her kitten; the
  girl asks to wear it, and Jida says: on your wedding day.

### Tale 6 — Tislit n Wanzar (the bride of Anzar)

*Why he is there:* the spring of Ath Abbas is dry. Teryel stole *Tislit n Wanzar*, the
ladle (aɣenja) the village dresses as a bride and carries from door to door to call the
rain, and hid it in the old cistern above the spring. **Goal: the ladle bride.**

- **Place:** the spring in a dry ravine at night: the fig tree, the stone cistern with
  its cracked wall, the well and its pulley.
- **Hunters:** her frogs (imqerqar). They crouch on their stone and leap at Mqidec once
  they have noticed him.
- **Mechanic:** water (he falls in, is held up, floats at the surface and launches again
  from it; a basin that rises and falls carries him) and two well buckets on one rope
  (his weight takes his bucket down and the other up; a stone landing hard in one throws
  whatever rests in the other).
- **Stages:** 21 *Tala* (the spring) · 22 *Asiṭel* (the bucket) · 23 *Lbir* (the well:
  buckets and water together) · 24 *Teryel deg tala*: break the cracked cistern wall; the
  flood carries Teryel down the ravine.
- **Ending:** the rain comes that same night; the girl hears it on the roof, and Jida
  sings the first line of the Anzar song with the children.

### Tale 7 — Ussan n tmeɣart (the old woman's borrowed days)

*Why he is there:* the legend Yennayer comes from. An old woman mocked January for a
mild winter and drove her goats up the mountain; January borrowed days from February and
froze her and her goats into rock on the Djurdjura. Teryel, her friend, ran off with the
old woman's spindle (izdi) and dropped it on the frozen pasture. Jida's grandmother spun
with it; her blanket is on the girl's knees. **Goal: the spindle.**

- **Place:** the high pasture in a January blizzard: the frozen pond, snowdrifts, snow
  cornices, the rocks that were goats.
- **Hunters:** the old woman's goats (iɣeṭṭen), half frozen. They charge along their
  ledge when Mqidec stands still on their level.
- **Mechanic:** the pond freezes on the wind's beat into slippery ice he can stand on,
  and thaws back into water; snowdrifts stop him dead and catch a charging goat, which
  then breaks at a touch.
- **Stages:** 25 *Agris* (the ice) · 26 *Adfel* (the snow) · 27 *Iɣeṭṭen* (the goats:
  ice, drifts and charges together) · 28 *Teryel deg wedfel*: knock the snow cornice;
  the avalanche fills the hollow, buries Teryel and leaves a snowbank to the door.
- **Ending:** the next morning is the first day of February and the snow stops; the
  girl asks if the old woman is still a rock, and Jida points at the Djurdjura.

## 5. Text and languages

- **Four languages** chosen on the title screen and in the pause menu: *Taqbaylit*,
  *Français*, *العربية*, *English*. Saved in the browser.
- English is the master. All lines live in one table keyed by id, with four strings
  each. Arabic is laid out right-to-left. Taqbaylit uses Latin letters with the usual
  Kabyle spelling (ɛ, ɣ, ḥ, ṭ, ḍ, ẓ, ṣ, č, ǧ); the Tifinagh font stays for titles and
  for ⵎⵇⵉⴷⴻⵛ. The user corrects the Taqbaylit drafts.
- Tale formulas and names stay in Taqbaylit in every language.

## 6. What this changes in the prototype

- Pumpkins become per-tale hunters: pots, spiders on threads, ijenniwen. The engine's
  hunter logic stays; each tale sets a hunter kind for art and sound.
- Stages regroup into tales of 4: three tales, 12 stages, replacing the 14. Most existing
  layouts move into their tale; the Weaver boss becomes three trap puzzles.
- Story screens become hearth scenes: Jida, the fire, a child, and the tale picture
  behind them.
- A language table and a selector replace the English-only `story.js`.
- The Owl is retired; the jackal is not used either, since Jida tells the tale.
